Introduction

QuickFleet runs same-day delivery on electric motorcycles in Abuja, with battery-swap hubs and a record for every parcel. We shaped the business with its founders, then designed and built the brand, the app and the website.

Strategy

Fuel went from ₦238 to ₦1,325 a litre between May 2023 and November 2026, and delivery prices followed. Most delivery is still informal: a price agreed on the phone, a rider nobody vetted, no record once the parcel leaves. We built the business around three promises that answer each one, then designed everything else to keep them.

  1. 01Fixed price

    Shown before you book and locked at pickup. Electric energy doesn’t move with the pump.

    A price agreed on the phone
  2. 02Known rider

    Vetted, trained and named before they arrive. QuickFleet’s riders, on QuickFleet’s machines.

    Whoever is free
  3. 03Proof at the door

    The receiver closes the delivery with a four-digit code, and every step is timestamped.

    No record after pickup

02

Branding

QuickFleet needed to look as dependable as the promise it makes: a fixed price, a known rider and proof at the door. Deep navy and purple carry the trust; teal and mint carry everything electric. The Q sends an arrow out of the circle, a parcel on its way, and runs from the boxes on the bikes to the app and the site.

03

Motion

A launch film that runs the whole system in under thirty seconds: the mark, the palette, the website, the app and the bikes. The same motion language carries into the site, where sections build in as you scroll.

04

Product

The customer app follows one parcel from home to the door. The price is shown before booking and locked at pickup, the rider is named and on the map, and the receiver closes the delivery with a four-digit code, from a text link, without the app.

  1. 01Start from home

    One button to send a parcel. Every rider is vetted and tracked to the door.

  2. 02Know who’s coming

    A named rider on the map, one minute away, with a number you can call.

  3. 03Proof at the door

    The receiver confirms with a four-digit code from a text link, and gets a receipt.

05

Website

quickfleet.co is built around what a customer asks first: what it costs, who comes and how it’s proved. Each page answers one of those, from the bikes and the hubs to the app, and a waitlist sorts customers, businesses and riders ahead of launch.

06

Operations

Battery-swap hubs, three to five in every city, close to where deliveries start. A rider swaps a flat battery for a charged one in minutes, so a parcel never waits on a charger, and the riders are QuickFleet’s own: trained, uniformed and on its machines.
