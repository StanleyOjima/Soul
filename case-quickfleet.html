Introduction

QuickFleet runs same-day delivery on electric motorcycles in Abuja, with battery-swap hubs and a record for every parcel. We shaped the business with its founders, then designed and built the brand, the app and the website.

Strategy

Fuel went from ₦238 to ₦1,325 a litre in three and a half years, and delivery prices followed. Most delivery is still informal: a price agreed on the phone, a rider nobody vetted, no record once the parcel leaves. We built the business around three promises that answer each one.

01 · The price

Fixed price

Shown before you book and locked at pickup. Electric energy doesn’t move with the pump.

A price agreed on the phone
02 · The rider

Known rider

Vetted, trained and named before they arrive. Our riders, on our machines.

02

Product

Six moments in the customer app, from home to the door. Each screen carries one of the three promises, so the customer sees the price, the rider and the proof without asking.

  1. 01 · Home

    Start from home.

    One button to send a parcel. Every rider is vetted, trained and tracked to the door.

  2. 02 · Where to

    Say where it’s going.

    Saved places and recent receivers come first, so a repeat delivery takes seconds.

  3. 03 · Price

    See the price. It’s locked.

    Route, distance and price before you book: ₦1,500, Garki to Maitama. It won’t move at pickup.

  4. 04 · Rider

    Know who’s coming.

    A named rider on a map, one minute away, with a number you can call.

  5. 05 · Proof

    The receiver closes it.

    They get a text link and confirm with a four-digit code. No app needed.

Brand

A mark and palette made for trust: deep navy and purple, with teal and mint for everything electric. The Q carries an arrow out of the circle, a parcel on its way. It runs from the bikes and boxes to the app and the site.

Website

quickfleet.co explains the network the way a customer meets it: what it costs, who comes and how it’s proved. A waitlist sorts customers, businesses and riders.

05

Operations

Battery-swap hubs, three to five in every city, close to where deliveries start. A rider swaps a flat battery for a charged one in minutes, so a parcel never waits on a charger.

  1. 01ArriveA rider rolls in with a flat battery.
  2. 02SwapA charged one goes in. Minutes, not hours.
